# Scoring research

How the conversion tables were sourced and what was derived from them.

## Source

*2026 key stage 2 scaled score conversion tables*, Standards and Testing Agency,
GOV.UK, published 16/07/2026.
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-tests-2026-scaled-scores/2026-key-stage-2-scaled-score-conversion-tables

Published as a web page rather than a spreadsheet, so the three tables (English GPS,
English reading, Mathematics) were extracted directly into
`seeds/seed_sats_conversion.csv` — one row per raw mark per paper, with a NULL
scaled score where none is awarded.

Stated on the page: a scaled score of 100 or above means the pupil met the expected
standard; the lowest awardable score is 80 and the highest is 120; a raw score of at
least 3 marks is needed to be awarded any scaled score.

## Two things this made visible

**The reading/GPS choice is low-risk.** The two curves stay within one scaled point
of each other across the whole range. Since `course_hierarchy` exposes no topic
names, English cannot be split into the two papers — but the cost of picking the
wrong one is about a point. An open assumption became a quantified one.

**Maths is compressed in the middle.** Raw marks 56–94 map to scaled 100–110, so
roughly a third of the mark range covers ten scaled points. Real differences in
maths ability will appear smaller than equivalent differences in English. A property
of the national test, not of this model, but it will prompt "why is everyone on 101?"

## Expected output shape

Cohort percentages of 58% (English) and 57.7% (Maths) put the typical pupil at
101–103. Year 6, at ~66%, lands around 103–105.

## Regenerating the seeds

```bash
python scripts/generate_conversion_seed.py
dbt seed --select seed_sats_conversion seed_sats_paper
```

The script asserts every figure in this document, plus the summary table in `docs/SCORING.md` §3,
before writing. If a documented number and the seed disagree it fails loudly
rather than picking a winner — which is how an impossible 66% row was caught:
it had reading at 106, but 65% and 66% of 50 marks both round to 33, so they cannot
produce different scores.

To refresh for a future year, take the new GOV.UK page, add its anchors under a new
`conversion_year`, and change the `conversion_year` var in `dbt_project.yml`. No
model logic changes.
